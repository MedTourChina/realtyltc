:::answer
Three conditions decide this layer: one self-use home, a documented year of work or study in China, and a purchase inside the city where that work or study is registered. Fail any one and the process ends here — which is much cheaper than failing it at a signing table.
:::

This is the layer where money is actually saved, because it is the only one with a binary answer. Everything after it is judgement. This is fact.

## The three conditions

**One home, for your own use.** Not two. Not one to live in and one to let. The entitlement is a single residential unit registered to you as an individual, and it is assessed at household level rather than personal level.

**A documented year in China.** You must have worked or studied here for at least twelve months, and you must be able to prove it with records a bureau can read — not with an employment letter alone.

**In the city where that work or study is registered.** Your Shanghai employment does not entitle you to buy in Hangzhou. Housing policy is municipal, so the city that grants permission is the city whose records contain you.

:::rule
If any of the three is shaky, resolve it before you view a property. A pending eligibility question is the most expensive thing to be carrying while you negotiate a deposit.
:::

## What "one self-use home" excludes

| Situation | Position |
| --- | --- |
| You own nothing, your spouse owns nothing | The entitlement is available |
| Your spouse owns a residential unit in China | The household has used the entitlement |
| You previously owned and sold | Usually used; confirm locally |
| Co-ownership with a relative | Counts as your ownership |
| Buying through a China-registered company | Not a self-use home; different rules, different taxes |
| Inheriting a property | Becomes your holding |

The household dimension surprises people. A foreign national married to a Chinese citizen who bought an apartment in 2014 before the marriage will often find that the entitlement is already used. This is not a rule you can argue with at the counter; it is a record.

## The year, measured in documents

Three records do the work, and they must agree with each other:

1. **Residence permit type.** Work or study. The bureau reads the category, not the expiry date. A five-year permit issued for family reunion is not a work permit.
2. **Employment or enrolment record.** Contract dates, employer name, employer's registered address.
3. **Tax or social insurance history.** Issued by the authority, not by your employer. This is the document that settles arguments.

Gaps between employers are the usual failure. Two years in the city with a three-month gap between jobs is not two years of continuous history, and whether the gap disqualifies you is a local judgement — which is exactly why the next section matters.

## The city lock

The employer's registered address, your social insurance account and the property must all sit in the same municipality. Three common situations that fail this test:

- Your employer is registered in Suzhou; you live and work in Shanghai. The Shanghai purchase does not qualify.
- You are employed by the Shanghai entity but permanently seconded to Beijing.
- You are a student in one city, employed remotely for a company in another.

:::warn
This is the condition most often resolved by asking, and most often assumed by people who then discover it at registration. If your arrangement is unusual, treat it as unavailable until a bureau confirms otherwise in writing.
:::

## Where to get an answer that counts

Not from an agent. Not from this site. Not from your HR department, though they are useful for documents.

- **The district housing bureau's service counter** (区住房保障和房屋管理局). Ask for your position in writing, and take the documents with you rather than describing them.
- **The property registration centre** (不动产登记中心). They will tell you what the registration step will require.
- **The tax service hall**, for the individual income tax record that proves continuity.

The verification page gives you the exact wording to use and what a satisfactory answer looks like: [how to verify](/verify/).

## When to stop

:::checklist
- Your permit is not work or study — stop, and fix the permit first
- You cannot produce twelve months of authority-issued income or contribution records — stop
- Your employer is registered in a different city from the one you want to buy in — stop
- Your spouse owns residential property in China — stop
- The unit is 40- or 50-year commercial stock — stop, this is layer 4 territory and usually unavailable to you
- Anyone tells you that you can buy anyway, for a fee — stop, and walk away from that person
:::

The honest summary of this layer: it is mostly administration, and administration is where foreign buyers lose the most time to the least purpose. Get the answer in writing before you do anything else.
