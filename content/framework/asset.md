:::answer
A Chinese title certificate describes a use-right over state land for a fixed term. 70 years is residential; 50 and 40 years are something else entirely — different utilities, different taxes, no school access, and generally closed to foreign individual buyers. Read four fields before you look at the kitchen.
:::

You never own land in China. You own a building and a right to use the land beneath it, for a term that starts at the land grant — years before the building was finished, and decades before you arrived.

## Three asset classes dressed as one

| | 70-year residential | 50-year institutional | 40-year commercial |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical product | Apartment, house, villa | Campus, mixed industrial | Office, loft, "apartment" |
| Foreign individual | Permitted, one unit | Generally not | Generally not |
| Utilities | Residential tariff | Commercial | Commercial, often 2–3× |
| School catchments | Yes | No | No |
| Deed tax | 1–2% | 3% | 3% |
| Mortgage | 25–30 years typical | Shorter, lower LTV | Often 50% LTV, 10 years |
| Renewal at expiry | Automatic in principle | Application | Application, premium likely |

The price gap between a 40-year loft and a 70-year apartment is not a bargain. It is the market pricing four different disadvantages at once.

## Reading the certificate

Four fields on the 不动产权证 decide most of what you need to know:

**权利性质 (nature of right).** Usually 出让 (granted) for a market purchase. 划拨 (allocated) land carries a top-up payment and is not the same asset.

**用途 (use).** 住宅 (residential), 商业 (commercial), 办公 (office). This single character changes the asset class.

**使用期限 (term).** The end date, not the duration. Subtract today's date to get what you are actually buying.

**面积 (area).** Registered area, which is what the tax authority uses — and which may differ from what the agent quotes.

:::warn
Ask for the land grant date (土地出让年限起始日) separately. A 2015 building on a 2008 grant has twelve years less than people assume, and agents routinely quote the completion date because it flatters the number.
:::

## The edges that cost real money

**140m².** Deed tax is 1% up to 140m² and 1.5–2% above it, on the whole price. A 141m² unit is not marginally more expensive than a 139m² unit; it is meaningfully more expensive to close. Between two comparable units either side of the line, the smaller one wins on arithmetic.

**Remaining term versus loan term.** Banks cap the mortgage term by the remaining land term as well as by your age. A unit with 28 years left will not support a 30-year loan, and the monthly payment changes accordingly.

**Management fee as a yield drag.** At ¥10/m² on 120m², that is ¥14,400 a year. On a 1.7% gross yield it is a sixth of the rent, before heating and vacancy.

**The heritage order.** In older central districts, a protection order can prevent structural change, external alteration, or even replacement windows. It is discoverable before you buy and almost impossible to reverse afterwards.

## The building, not just the unit

Three building-level facts matter more than the unit's finish:

- **Completion year and lift replacement.** A 2005 tower with original lifts has a capital call coming.
- **Management quality.** Ask residents, not the office. Fee collection rate is a reasonable proxy for whether anyone is actually running the place.
- **Ownership mix.** A building full of short-term lets behaves differently at 2am than one full of owner-occupiers.

## What to check, and where

:::checklist
- Title certificate: nature of right, use, term end date, area
- Land grant contract: the start date of the term
- Planning permission and any heritage or demolition order
- Management fee schedule and the last three years of collection rate
- Utility tariff classification, in writing from the utility
- Building completion date and lift replacement history
:::

Where each of those comes from is set out on [how to verify](/verify/).

## When to stop

:::checklist
- Remaining land term under 35 years — stop
- Use is anything other than residential — stop, and re-read layer 1
- 划拨 land without a clear top-up arrangement — stop
- A heritage or demolition order on the building — stop
- Management fee above 1.5% of price per year — stop and recompute the yield
- Registered area within 3m² of the 140m² threshold — slow down and do the arithmetic
:::
