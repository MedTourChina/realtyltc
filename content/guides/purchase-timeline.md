:::answer
Six to ten weeks for resale with cash, eight to twelve with a mortgage, counted from the accepted offer rather than from the day you start looking. Off-plan runs two to three years and is not the same transaction at all.
:::

The timeline question is asked in the wrong unit. People ask "how long does buying take", when the answer depends on three things that have nothing to do with the buyer: whether the seller has a mortgage to clear, whether the bank's valuation matches the price, and how quickly the district can give you a tax and registration appointment.

Here is what actually happens, in order, with the durations I have seen rather than the ones in the brochure.

## Before week one: the file

Nothing on this list expires quickly, and every item on it has stopped a purchase at the registration counter:

- Passport, valid for more than six months, with the residence permit inside it.
- Work permit (外国人工作许可证) or enrolment certificate.
- Individual income tax records or social insurance records covering twelve continuous months, issued by the authority rather than printed from a phone.
- Proof of the employment address, if your employer's registered address differs from where you work.
- Bank statements for the down payment, six months, showing where the money came from.
- A written pre-approval from a bank, if you are borrowing. Not a conversation — a letter.

The tax and social insurance records are the item that fails most often, and they fail quietly: twelve months of records that cover a period in another city, or eleven months and a gap, or contributions made by a third-party agency rather than the employer. Get the records before you view anything, so the gap is discovered while there is still time to fix it.

## Weeks one to three: search and verification

Viewing takes as long as you let it. The part that takes real time is verifying the district and the compound — the commute measurement, the school answer, the resale pool — which is covered in the guide on choosing where to live.

Do not compress this. A purchase that moves quickly because you liked the second flat you saw is a purchase that will spend weeks four to twelve discovering things it should have known in week two.

## Week three or four: the offer and the deposit

Chinese resale practice runs on a deposit — 定金, dìng jīn — usually 1–5% of price, paid against a written deposit agreement rather than the sale contract. The deposit is what makes the offer binding, and its refund conditions are the single most important sentence in the whole transaction.

Three clauses to insist on, in writing:

1. **The deposit is refunded in full if eligibility fails.** Not "if the buyer is unable to proceed" — the specific case, named, with a date by which the answer will be known.
2. **The deposit is refunded if the bank valuation comes in below the contract price and the buyer does not wish to cover the difference in cash.** Without this, a low valuation turns into an obligation to find money in a week.
3. **The deposit sits in the supervised account** — 资金监管, zī jīn jiān guǎn — from the day it is paid, not in the seller's personal account "as a holding".

A seller who will not accept escrow for the deposit is telling you something. Believe it.

## Week four or five: online contract registration

This is the step that formally tests your eligibility, and it is the gate that most foreign buyers do not know exists until they reach it.

The sale is entered into the housing authority's system — 网签, wǎng qiān — which locks the unit against a second sale and runs your documents against the purchase restriction rules. If your twelve months are in the wrong city, or your permit type is wrong, this is where it surfaces. It is also why the deposit agreement needs the refund clause: the failure happens after the money has moved.

In most cities this takes a day once the documents are accepted. Getting the documents accepted can take a week.

## Weeks five to nine: the mortgage, if any

A foreign buyer's loan goes through the same pipeline as a domestic one, with two additions: the bank wants the eligibility documents, and the loan-to-value is usually capped lower — 50–60% is common, against 70–80% for a first-time domestic buyer.

The bank then sends a valuer. The valuation is the bank's view of what the flat is worth, and it is frequently below the contract price, especially where the price has been negotiated upward or the compound has few recent comparables. If the valuation is 10% low, the bank lends against the valuation, and you owe the difference in cash, on a deadline.

Approval typically takes two to four weeks. Add a week if the seller still has a mortgage, because the discharge has to be arranged before the bank will release funds against a clean title.

## Weeks eight to ten: tax and registration

The tax appointment is where the transaction becomes arithmetic. Deed tax — 契税, qì shuì — is assessed on the registered price, and the rate depends on area and on whether this is your first home. In the current national schedule (in force since the 2024 adjustment, still applied in March 2026):

- 1% up to 140m² for a first or second home
- 1.5% above 140m² for a first home
- 2% above 140m² for a second home
- 3% for a third home or commercial stock

There is also the seller's side: VAT at around 5.3% on a resale held under two years, and individual income tax at 1% of price or 20% of the gain, exempt where the property has been the seller's only home for five years or more — 满五唯一, mǎn wǔ wéi yī.

Who pays what is negotiable, and in practice the buyer often pays the seller's taxes because the seller quotes a net price. Agree this in writing before the deposit, not at the tax window.

## Weeks ten to twelve: transfer and handover

Transfer — 过户, guò hù — is the appointment at the 不动产登记中心 where the new certificate is issued. The escrowed balance is released against the new certificate, not against a promise, which is the entire reason escrow exists.

Then, in the week after:

- Read the water, electricity and gas meters and transfer the accounts.
- Settle the property management fee — 物业费, wù yè fèi — to the day of handover, and get the settlement in writing from the management office, because unpaid arrears follow the property.
- Collect the maintenance fund — 维修基金 — receipts, if the seller has them.
- Register your residence at the local police station — 住宿登记, zhù sù dēng jì — within the required window. Hotels do this automatically; private ownership does not.

## The five things that delay almost every transaction

1. **The seller's mortgage discharge.** Two to six weeks, and nothing else can proceed. Ask for the discharge certificate before you pay a deposit.
2. **The bank valuation gap.** Discovered late, payable in days.
3. **A spouse who has to sign and is not in the country.** A notarised power of attorney takes weeks to produce and must be accepted by the registration office, not just by the agent.
4. **Eligibility records that do not match.** The one-year rule, the city, the employer's registered address.
5. **A tax assessment the buyer disputes.** Rare, but it stops the clock completely while it is resolved.

## Off-plan is a different transaction

A new unit bought before completion is not a faster version of the above. It is two to three years, with stage payments, a developer rather than a seller, and a pre-sale permit — 预售许可证, yù shòu xǔ kě zhèng — that must exist before you pay anything. If the permit does not cover the building you are buying, the payment is unprotected.

The tax clock also runs differently: for the five-year exemption, the count usually starts from the certificate date, not the contract date, so an off-plan purchase can be eight years old before it qualifies.

:::kill
Walk away when the seller refuses supervised escrow for the deposit; when there is no pre-sale permit for the building you are paying for; when the deposit agreement has no refund on eligibility failure; when the bank's valuation gap would leave you without the cash to close and the seller will not extend; or when anyone asks you to register a price lower than the one you are paying.
:::

:::checklist
Before you pay a deposit: tax and social insurance records in hand for twelve continuous months in this city · written bank pre-approval, with the loan-to-value stated · deposit agreement with the three refund clauses · escrow account details in writing · seller's mortgage discharge arranged or scheduled · who pays which tax written down · a completion date that assumes the discharge takes six weeks · the decision sheet filled in for layers four and five.
:::
