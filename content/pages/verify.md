:::answer
Assume nothing you were told verbally, and assume nothing on this website either. Every important fact has a document behind it and an office that issues it. Your job is to get the document, in writing, from the office — not to get a better opinion from a second agent.
:::

The [framework](/framework/) tells you what to decide. This page tells you how to check whether what you have been told is true, because in this market the gap between what an agent says and what the registry records is where the money goes.

## The principle: documents over opinions

There are two ways to answer "can I buy this?"

- **Opinion:** "Foreigners can buy here, my colleague did it last year." Free, fast, unreliable, and the source is paid when you transact.
- **Document:** a written reply from the district housing bureau, based on your passport and your permits, stating what they require from you.

Only the second one is worth anything. Every section below is about producing documents rather than collecting reassurances.

## Six questions that reveal a bad deal

Ask each one plainly, and watch what happens to the conversation.

:::checklist
1. **"Can I see the title certificate, and the land grant date?"** If the answer is a photograph of a certificate with the numbers obscured, the deal is not ready. See [the due diligence checklist](/guides/due-diligence-checklist/).
2. **"Which building is this unit actually in?"** Duplicate listings built from a nicer unit elsewhere are the standard opening move of the low end of the market.
3. **"Who is the registered owner, and will their spouse sign?"** Evasion here is a title problem, not a scheduling problem. This is where [marriage, divorce and inheritance](/guides/marriage-divorce-and-inheritance/) stops most deals.
4. **"Can you print the registered transaction prices for this building for the last six months?"** Listing prices are marketing. Registered prices are facts. What counts as a comparable is in [new build versus resale](/guides/new-build-vs-resale-costs/).
5. **"What is the remaining land term, and how does it affect a mortgage?"** A confident answer with a number is a good sign. "It doesn't matter" is not. The terms are in [seventy, fifty and forty years](/guides/seventy-fifty-forty-year-land-use/).
6. **"What happens to my deposit if my eligibility check fails?"** If the answer is not in the contract, the answer is that you lose it. See [escrow and payment stages](/guides/escrow-and-payment-stages/).
:::

## The counter script

Go to the district housing bureau (区住房保障和房屋管理局) in person, with your documents. Ask these questions in this order. Chinese first, then what you are listening for.

Every question below is testing a condition set out in the [Eligibility layer](/framework/eligibility/) — if an answer contradicts that page, believe the counter and treat the page as out of date.

**"请问外籍个人在本市购买一套自用住房,需要提交哪些材料?"**
*Which documents does a foreign individual need to submit to buy one self-use home in this city?*

> You are listening for a list, not a verdict. Write it down. If the answer is "ask your agent", ask again and say you are the buyer.

**"我在本市工作已满一年,需要提供哪些证明材料?社保和个税记录在哪里打印?"**
*I have worked in this city for over a year — which proof is required, and where do I obtain the social insurance and tax records?*

> A specific office name and a specific document name is the good answer. What the [one-year rule](/guides/the-one-year-rule/) actually requires is on that page.

**"我的劳动合同签约单位注册地在外地,是否可以?"**
*My employment contract is with a company registered in another city — is that acceptable?*

> This is the city-lock question, and it is the one most likely to produce a "no".

**"如果我已婚,配偶名下已有住宅,我是否还可以购买?"**
*If I am married and my spouse already owns residential property, may I still buy?*

> Watch whether they ask for the marriage certificate before answering. Why that question is decisive is in [marriage, divorce and inheritance](/guides/marriage-divorce-and-inheritance/).

**"能否出具书面答复或材料清单?"**
*Can you issue a written reply or a document checklist?*

> This is the whole exercise. A printed checklist with an official stamp is worth more than an hour of conversation.

:::note
Take a Chinese-speaking colleague or a notarised translator if you can. Not because the staff are unhelpful — they are usually patient — but because a misheard condition at this stage becomes a rejected registration six weeks later.
:::

## Checking what a seller tells you

Three claims are worth verifying independently, because each one is easy to state and hard to disprove later:

**"The area is 118m²."** The registered area is on the certificate and is what the tax authority uses. A 3m² difference near the 140m² threshold changes your deed tax by half a percentage point of the entire price — the bands are set out in [the full cost of buying](/guides/the-full-cost-of-buying-a-home-in-china/).

**"The building is from 2015."** The land grant date is usually earlier, and the remaining term is what a bank will underwrite against. The arithmetic of what is left is in [seventy, fifty and forty years](/guides/seventy-fifty-forty-year-land-use/).

**"The management fee is low."** Ask the management office for the fee schedule and the collection rate. A low fee with a 60% collection rate means the building is being run on borrowed time. What a normal fee buys is in [property tax and running costs](/guides/property-tax-in-china/).

## When you cannot verify something

Sometimes the answer genuinely is not available: a registry office that will not search without the owner present, a seller who will not release the certificate before a deposit.

In those cases the correct move is not to proceed on trust. It is to change the structure:

- Make the payment conditional, in writing, on the document arriving by a date
- Use a [supervised escrow account](/glossary/#supervised-escrow) so the money is not at risk while you wait — the mechanics are in [escrow and payment stages](/guides/escrow-and-payment-stages/)
- Walk away if the structure is refused

A seller who will not accept a conditional payment is telling you something about the state of the title.

## What good looks like

A clean file is boring. The checklist from the bureau matches what your lawyer asked for, the title search comes back with no mortgages, the registered price list supports the price you agreed, and the deposit sits in an account that neither party can empty alone.

If any part of that is exciting, slow down. The framework exists so that the excitement happens before the money, not after it.
