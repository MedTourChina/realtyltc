:::answer
Plan for 3–6% above the headline price on a cash purchase, and up to 9% if the seller's taxes are pushed onto you. On a ¥10M apartment that is an extra ¥300,000 to ¥900,000.
:::

Buyers negotiate hard on the price and then lose the same money on the closing costs, because the costs are spread across five different parties and nobody presents them together. Here they are, together.

## Deed tax (契税)

The main acquisition tax, paid by the buyer at registration. Rates changed on 1 December 2024 and the current structure is simpler than the pre-2024 one:

| Your position | Area ≤ 140m² | Area > 140m² |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First home | 1% | 1.5% |
| Second home | 1% | 2% |
| Third home or more | 3% (statutory rate, local variation) | 3% |

Foreign nationals buying their single permitted self-use home are normally assessed at the first-home rate. The reduction is applied at registration, not negotiated with the seller.

:::note
The 140m² threshold is a cliff edge, not a gradient. A 141m² unit is taxed at 1.5% on the whole price, not 1.5% on the excess. Where two comparable units sit either side of the line, the smaller one is materially cheaper to close.
:::

## The other buyer-side costs

| Item | Typical amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency commission | 1–2% of price | Buyer side is usually around 1% and negotiable |
| Notarisation | 0.1–0.3%, or a fixed ¥1,000–3,000 | Required for many foreign-buyer transactions |
| Registration fee | ¥80 | Residential; trivial but unavoidable |
| Translation | ¥500–2,000 | Contracts and certificates for your own records and lender |
| Legal due diligence | ¥8,000–35,000 | Independent title and contract review |
| Mortgage arrangement | ¥0–5,000, plus valuation | If borrowing |
| Property management deposit | 3–6 months of fees | Paid at handover, refundable at exit |
| Renovation | ¥3,000–8,000/m² | The real wildcard; older stock needs more |

## The seller-side taxes that migrate

Officially, value-added tax and individual income tax are the seller's liability. In practice, in a seller's market, a seller quotes a net price and the buyer absorbs them. Ask explicitly, and get the answer in the contract:

**VAT (增值税).** Around 5.3% of the transaction price where the property has been held for less than two years. Held for two years or more, it is generally exempt — with additional conditions in the four tier-one cities for larger or non-ordinary units.

**Individual income tax (个人所得税).** Typically 1% of price, or 20% of the gain where that is higher. Exempt where the property has been the seller's only home for five years or more (满五唯一).

Combined, these can add more than 6% to what you actually pay. That is the difference between a good purchase and a bad one, and it is entirely visible before you sign.

## A worked example

A ¥10,000,000 resale apartment in Shanghai, 118m², cash purchase, seller pays their own taxes:

| Item | Amount | Cumulative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase price | ¥10,000,000 | ¥10,000,000 |
| Deed tax at 1% | ¥100,000 | ¥10,100,000 |
| Agency commission at 1% | ¥100,000 | ¥10,200,000 |
| Notarisation at 0.15% | ¥15,000 | ¥10,215,000 |
| Registration | ¥80 | ¥10,215,080 |
| Translation and admin | ¥2,000 | ¥10,217,080 |
| Legal due diligence | ¥20,000 | ¥10,237,080 |
| **Total** | | **¥10,237,080 (2.4% on top)** |

Now change two assumptions. The same purchase at 150m² pushes deed tax to 1.5% (¥150,000), and a seller who wants the buyer to absorb VAT and income tax adds roughly 6.3% (¥630,000). Total on top: about **8.8%**.

## What this means for your budget

:::checklist
- Set your maximum price 6% below your available cash, not at it
- Ask the seller, in writing, who bears VAT and individual income tax
- Confirm the registered area before you fall in love with a unit — 140m² is a tax cliff
- Get the title search and contract review done before the deposit, not after
- Keep a 10% contingency for renovation on anything older than fifteen years
:::

## The cost that is not in the table

Liquidity. A resale in a thin market can take six to twelve months to clear, and the round-trip cost of buying and selling inside two years — deed tax, commission, VAT, income tax — can exceed 10%. If your horizon is short, renting is not a compromise; it is arithmetic.
