:::answer
No national residential property tax. Two pilot cities — Shanghai and Chongqing — tax narrow categories of housing, mostly second homes and luxury units. Everything else you have read about a coming national tax is still a proposal.
:::

Property tax is the topic most likely to be explained to a foreign buyer by someone who has read a headline rather than a statute. Here is the actual state of play.

## What exists today

**The 1986 Provisional Regulations on Property Tax.** This taxes buildings used for business, levied on the residual value of the property at 1.2%, or on rental income at 12%. It is a commercial tax. Owner-occupied residential property has been outside it.

**The 2011 pilots in Shanghai and Chongqing.** Two municipalities were authorised to tax certain residential holdings. The schemes are local, narrow and materially different from each other.

**Everything else is a proposal.** A national residential property tax has been discussed, piloted in rhetoric, and legislated in draft for more than a decade. It has not been enacted. Anyone telling you it arrives next quarter is guessing.

## Shanghai, in practice

The Shanghai pilot targets:

- Second and subsequent homes bought by Shanghai-registered households
- Homes bought by non-Shanghai households, including foreign buyers, subject to exemptions
- Taxable value is set at 70% of the assessed market price
- The rate is 0.4% or 0.6%, depending on whether the price exceeds twice the city's average new-home price
- A per-capita area allowance of 60m² applies to Shanghai households

For a foreign buyer purchasing a single self-use home, the pilot is generally not engaged: the scheme is aimed at holdings beyond the household's basic residence. That said, "generally" is doing work in that sentence, and the assessment is made by the local tax bureau against your file, not against a general rule.

:::warn
Do not take an agent's assurance that "foreigners do not pay property tax". Ask the district tax bureau to confirm your position in writing, based on your specific household and the unit you are buying. The exemption analysis depends on facts that a salesperson may not have.
:::

## Chongqing, in practice

Chongqing's pilot is aimed at high-value and luxury properties — particularly expensive villas and large units above a price threshold — rather than at second homes generally. Rates run roughly 0.5% to 1.2%. If you are buying an ordinary apartment in Chongqing, the pilot is unlikely to reach you. If you are buying a villa above the threshold, it will.

## What you actually pay every year

Property tax is not the recurring cost that matters for most owners. These are:

| Cost | Amount | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property management fee | ¥3–15/m² per month | Monthly or quarterly |
| Heating (north only) | ¥20–30/m² per season | Annual |
| Maintenance fund (维修基金) | 2–3% of price on new homes | One-off at purchase |
| Insurance | Optional, ¥1,000–3,000 | Annual |
| Property tax | Pilot cities only, narrow scope | Annual where applicable |

For a 120m² apartment at ¥8/m² management fee, that is ¥11,520 a year before anything else. On a 1.6% gross yield, management fees alone consume a fifth of the rent.

## What to do

:::checklist
- Ask the district tax bureau, not the agent, whether the pilot applies to your purchase
- Get the management fee and heating cost in writing before you offer
- Budget 2–3% for the maintenance fund on any new-build purchase
- Keep the tax receipts — they are part of the file you will need at resale and repatriation
- Revisit the position annually; local rules have changed with little notice
:::

## The forward view

If a national residential property tax is eventually enacted, the design most often discussed is a per-household area allowance with a progressive rate above it — which would leave a single self-use apartment largely untouched and make the second and third homes expensive to hold.

For a foreign buyer limited to one home, that is a favourable asymmetry. It is also a reason not to build a portfolio through nominee arrangements.
