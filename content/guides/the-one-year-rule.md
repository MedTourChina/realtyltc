:::answer
One year of documented work or study in China, in the same city where you are buying, evidenced by permits and records — not by a letter from your employer alone.
:::

The rule is short. The failure mode is not: buyers assume that because they have lived in China for years, the requirement is satisfied, then discover at contract registration that the record they can produce covers eleven months, or covers a different city, or covers employment that was not properly registered.

## What counts

**Registered employment.** A work permit (外国人工作许可证) held by you, an employment contract with a China-registered entity, and a corresponding record of individual income tax or social insurance contributions. This is the strongest and most common basis.

**Full-time study.** Enrolment at a Chinese institution, evidenced by a student residence permit and a certificate of enrolment. Study counts toward the year, and the purchase must still be in the city of study.

**Mixed periods.** Studying for six months and working for eight months in the same city usually adds up, but the two records have to be linkable to you and to the same municipality. Expect to be asked to explain the gap.

## What does not count

- Time on a tourist (L), business (M) or transit visa
- Informal or cash-in-hand work without a work permit
- Remote employment for a foreign employer while you live in China, unless that employment has been properly registered
- Time spent in another Chinese city, even for the same employer
- Internships that were never registered as study or employment

:::warn
Remote work for an overseas employer is the most common trap. You may have lived in Shanghai for three years, but if your income comes from abroad and your residence is not tied to local employment or study, the housing bureau has nothing to record you against. Fix the record before you start viewing property, not after.
:::

## How it is verified

Three checks, in order of strictness:

**Permit type and validity.** The residence permit must reflect work or study. The bureau reads the permit category, not just the expiry date.

**Employment or enrolment continuity.** Contract dates, employer name and registered address are compared with social insurance or tax records. Gaps between employers are the usual problem.

**City match.** The employer's registered address, your social insurance account and the property must all be in the same municipality. If your employer is registered in Suzhou and you work from Shanghai, the purchase in Shanghai will not qualify.

## Building the evidence file

:::checklist
- Passport and current residence permit, plus previous permits if the qualifying period crosses renewals
- Work permit card or notice, in date order
- Employment contract, including any renewal or amendment pages
- Individual income tax records from the tax authority, or social insurance contribution history
- Employer letter stating your role, start date and registered office address in the city
- For students: enrolment certificate and student residence permit
- Marriage certificate if married, notarised and translated if issued abroad
:::

The tax or social insurance record is the document that does the work. It is issued by the authority, not by your employer, and it is much harder to argue with. Get it early: some districts issue it over the counter, some only through a portal you need a local account to access.

## Timing it correctly

The qualifying year is measured at the point of contract registration, not at the point you start looking. That means a buyer who is two months short can usually resolve it by waiting; a buyer who is two months short and has already paid a deposit is in a negotiation with a seller who has no reason to be patient.

:::note
If you are close to the threshold, do not sign anything with a payment attached until the record is in hand and has been seen by whoever will file your registration. Ask for the record to be issued in the week before signing, not the week after.
:::

## If you do not qualify

There are three honest routes, and no clever fourth one:

1. **Wait.** If you are short on time and staying in China, this is usually the correct answer.
2. **Buy through a China-registered company.** Legitimate for business premises, awkward and tax-inefficient for a home, and it does not create a personal entitlement.
3. **Rent.** A twelve-month lease costs a fraction of a purchase and lets you test the district before you commit capital.

The route we would advise against is the one that involves someone else's name on the certificate. Structures built on a nominee arrangement are common, undocumented, and extremely difficult to unwind — particularly when a relationship changes or an heir appears.
