:::answer
Yes — one self-use home, in the city where you work or study, after at least one year in China. No land, no second home, and no national application process: your district housing bureau decides.
:::

Almost every English-language summary of this question is accurate at the national level and nearly useless at the desk where the decision is actually made. The national framework sets three conditions. Each city then interprets those conditions through its own administrative practice, and that practice is what you will be asked to satisfy with paper.

## The three conditions, stated plainly

**One home, for self-use.** Not two, not one for investment and one to live in. The entitlement is a single residential unit, and it is registered to you as an individual. If you are married, the entitlement is assessed at the household level.

**At least one year of work or study in China.** This is a real requirement, checked against permits and records rather than asserted in a form. We cover how it is verified in [the one-year rule](/guides/the-one-year-rule/).

**In the city where you work or study.** You cannot use your Shanghai employment to buy in Hangzhou, or your Beijing posting to buy in Shanghai. Since housing policy is municipal, the city that grants the permission is the city where your employment is registered.

## What you can and cannot buy

| Property type | Foreign individual | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential, 70-year land term | Yes — one unit | The standard route |
| Residential, single-family house | Yes — one unit | Rare in central areas; villa compounds on city edges |
| 商住 / loft, 40 or 50-year term | Generally no | Closed to foreign individuals in most cities |
| Office or retail unit | Generally no | Typically purchased through a company |
| Land | Never | Land is state-owned; you acquire the building and the land-use right |
| Subsidised or policy housing | No | Restricted to eligible local households |
| Foreclosure auction (法拍) | Sometimes | Eligibility must be confirmed before bidding, not after |

:::warn
A seller or agent who tells you that a 40-year loft, a hotel-style apartment or an office unit is "fine for foreigners" is either misinformed or selling you something they cannot deliver. Ask the district housing bureau directly, in writing, before any payment.
:::

## How the one-home rule is actually checked

The check happens twice: once informally when you view and negotiate, and once formally at contract registration (网签), where your identity and eligibility are entered into the housing authority's system.

Three practical points that surprise buyers:

1. **A spouse's Chinese property counts.** If your husband or wife already owns an apartment in China, the household may exceed the entitlement even though you personally own nothing.
2. **Registration records are increasingly portable.** City-level records were historically siloed. Cross-city and national checks have tightened considerably since 2023, so a property registered in another province is now much harder to leave out of a declaration.
3. **Joint ownership is allowed, the total is not.** You and your spouse may both be registered on the certificate. That is still one household entitlement, not two.

## Documents you will need

Requirements vary, but a complete file usually contains:

:::checklist
- Passport, with a valid residence permit for work or study
- Foreigner work permit (外国人工作许可证) or enrolment certificate
- Employment contract, and social insurance or individual income tax records showing the qualifying period
- Marriage certificate, notarised and translated if issued outside China
- Proof of a Chinese address, and the police registration (住宿登记) that goes with it
- Bank statements or a funding letter, if a mortgage is involved
- A signed statement that you hold no other residential property in China
:::

## City differences

The national framework is uniform. The administrative temperature is not.

| City | What to expect beyond the national requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Shanghai | Strict document review; notarised documents for anything issued abroad; social insurance continuity checked |
| Beijing | Most literal interpretation; household property declaration required for married applicants |
| Shenzhen | Social insurance and tax records verified electronically; restricted-resale windows checked |
| Guangzhou | District-level rules differ inside the city; confirm the specific district |
| Hangzhou | Close checks on social insurance continuity, including gaps between employers |
| Suzhou | Industrial Park and historic districts are administered separately |
| Chengdu, Wuhan, Tianjin | National framework applied with more local discretion; ask before assuming |
| Qingdao, Xiamen | Additional local restrictions have changed repeatedly; verify current practice |

## Four misconceptions worth killing now

**"Buying property gives me a visa or residence status."** It does not. Property ownership confers no immigration benefit. Your status is driven by employment, study, family or investment categories entirely separate from housing.

**"I can buy in any city I like."** You cannot. The purchase must be in the city of your employment or study, and that is checked.

**"A company can buy the home and I can live in it."** A China-registered company can own property, but it is a business asset. Using a company to get around the individual limit creates a structure that is examined, and often unwound, at resale.

**"One year means one year of residence, loosely."** It means documented work or study, in the same city, for a qualifying period. Month-counting matters, and it is the single most common reason a purchase stalls at the registration desk.

## What to do next

:::checklist
- Confirm your qualifying period and city before you look at a single property
- Ask the district housing bureau, in writing, what documents they will require from you specifically
- Have your marriage certificate notarised and translated now, not at contract stage
- Get a written title and eligibility opinion before paying any deposit
- Budget for the full transaction cost, not just the price
:::
